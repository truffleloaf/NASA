# 60-second demonstration

Open the landing page with motion enabled on a WebGL-capable browser. For a reproducible
run use `/?view=explorer#v=1&site=de-gerlache&start=2026-11-10&end=2026-11-30&sun=12&gap=0&duration=18&mast=2&hour=360`.

- **0–8 s — The question.** “A lunar mission needs both sunlight for power and a view of
  Earth. This tool finds their overlap.” Show the Moon and galaxy; open the explorer.
- **8–20 s — The answer.** Show de Gerlache and its estimated 101-hour window. Select the
  green window track. “Every result says where it comes from. These are estimates, not
  flight clearance.” Open the provenance explanation.
- **20–32 s — The terrain.** Scrub time so shadows change. Switch to Surface and Face Sun;
  point out the horizon and paths. Return to Polar and inspect a terrain point.
- **32–42 s — The decision.** Raise the antenna mast, observe the Earth-link estimate,
  then compare sites or two planning periods on the same time scale.
- **42–52 s — The limits.** Show custom coordinates: nearby sites are references, and
  uncovered locations do not invent results. Open Methods & data for sources.
- **52–60 s — The handoff.** Export the brief, show the limits, top window and source
  footer. “A small, offline planning tool that makes its evidence and uncertainty visible.”

Timing is a presentation guide. The source numbers above are estimated from the bundled
2026 ephemeris and horizon data at the linked settings. Avoid claiming the illustrative
landing scene is the selected-date simulation or that a visible Earth guarantees a radio link.
